# Research and evidence register

The strongest research implication is to combine meaningful problems with structured support and independent checks of understanding. No cited source establishes that Kodergarden's proposed ordering, garden mechanics, or mastery thresholds will improve learning. Those are design hypotheses evaluated in [G](G-classroom-experiments.md).

Sources were consulted on 13 October 2026. This is a focused design review, not a systematic literature review. Primary papers, research syntheses, author or university copies, and official platform materials are used. Historical curriculum examples are identified as such; their mechanics are not treated as current product inventories or efficacy studies.

## Instructional evidence

| ID and source | Finding or position and evidence limits | Application to Kodergarden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 · Brennan and Resnick, 2012, [New frameworks for studying and assessing computational thinking](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/new-frameworks-for-studying-and-assessing-the-development-of-computational-thinking/) | Research framework distinguishes concepts, practices, and perspectives; proposes portfolio analysis, artifact interviews, and design scenarios. The institutional abstract was read; the linked full PDF was unavailable. It is not a randomized comparison of curricula. | Assess how a learner predicts, tests, explains, and changes a program, alongside the constructs in it. Interview a learner about an actual artifact; do not infer understanding from block presence. |
| S2 · Alfieri, Brooks, Aldrich, and Tenenbaum, 2011, [Does Discovery-Based Instruction Enhance Learning?](https://app.nova.edu/toolbox/instructionalproducts/edd8124/articles/2011-Alfieri_et_al.pdf), abstract and discussion pp. 10–12 | Two meta-analyses covering 164 studies distinguish unassisted discovery from supported discovery. Results favor explicit instruction over unassisted discovery and assisted discovery over its comparison conditions. Tasks, ages, and interventions vary. | Use a short encounter with the problem, followed by guidance, examples, feedback, and explanation. Do not withhold the construct until a child becomes frustrated. |
| S3 · Sentance, Waite, and Kallia, 2019, [Teaching computer programming with PRIMM](https://primmportal.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/teaching-computer-programming-with-primm-a-sociocultural-perspective.pdf), abstract and study sections | Mixed-methods study in 13 schools with 493 learners aged 11–14 over 8–12 weeks reported better post-test performance than a comparison group. PRIMM organizes Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make. Context and study design limit causal and platform-wide generalization. | Make code reading and peer discussion normal before independent construction. Our proposed short block lessons are an adaptation, not a replication of that study. |
| S4 · Papert and Harel, 1991, [Situating Constructionism](https://pirun.ku.ac.th/~btun/papert/sitcons.pdf), opening and examples | Theoretical account emphasizes constructing shareable artifacts and personal meanings. It does not establish a universal numerical effect or prescribe a single progression. | Include learner-authored gardens and strategy explanations that classmates can inspect. A succession of externally specified mazes alone provides little authorship. |
| S5 · Sweller, 1988, [Cognitive Load During Problem Solving](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4), abstract | Modeling and experimental work argues that problem-solving search can consume processing capacity needed for schema acquisition. The original tasks are not this tablet programming environment. | Separate a new world rule, a new control construct, and a new editor gesture. Fade worked programs and reduce irrelevant animation. Our two-level nesting default is a usability hypothesis, not a research-derived limit. |
| S6 · Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn, 2007, [Scaffolding and Achievement in Problem-Based and Inquiry Learning](https://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/EDUC220/ThinkPaper/HmeloSilverDuncan2007.pdf), pp. 99–107 | Scholarly response explains that problem-based and inquiry approaches can be extensively scaffolded; it disputes equating them with unguided discovery. It is an argument drawing on studies, not a direct trial of garden puzzles. | Have children propose a rule, test a counterexample, and revise it with teacher prompts. A real problem can precede the formal block without removing instructional support. |
| S7 · National Research Council, 2000, [How People Learn, Chapter 3, Learning and Transfer](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9853/chapter/6), especially pp. 62–66 and summary | Research synthesis describes the importance of initial understanding, context variation, and abstracting relevant features for transfer. Success in one setting does not guarantee use elsewhere. | Distinguish a rotated maze, a new family of mazes, and a non-navigation task. Assess these separately. Several fixed layouts give evidence of within-family robustness, not proof of universal reasoning. |
| S8 · Kulik, Kulik, and Bangert-Drowns, 1990, [Effectiveness of Mastery Learning Programs](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/kulik_kulik_Bangert-Drowns_1990.pdf), abstract and discussion | Meta-analysis of 108 controlled evaluations reports positive examination effects, with variation across procedures and content and possible additional instructional time. It does not validate software completion locks or a particular passing percentage. | Use corrective support and fresh checks. Allow teacher-managed advancement and enrichment while collecting missing evidence. Do not hold a whole class at one puzzle until everyone earns an efficiency star. |
| S9 · Black and Wiliam, 1998, [Assessment and Classroom Learning](https://assess.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2019-02/blackwiliam_1998.pdf), abstract and feedback discussion | Review connects formative assessment with feedback used to improve teaching and learning. Heterogeneous interventions do not justify a guaranteed effect from adding a dashboard. | Evidence must change the next action: revisit orientation, compare branches, or inspect state updates. A score without a response is insufficient. |
| S10 · Clark, Tanner-Smith, and Killingsworth, 2016, [Digital Games, Design, and Learning](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654315582065), abstract and inclusion criteria | Meta-analysis finds average learning advantages for digital games and variation by design. It excludes interventions primarily teaching students to create/program games. | Treat game rules as part of instruction and test their contribution. Attractive scenery, engagement, and completion are not evidence of programming transfer. Its pooled effect is not a forecast for Kodergarden. |

## Synthesis and boundaries

The following are Kodergarden design hypotheses informed by the sources, rather than findings reported in those studies:

- A visible pair of contrasting worlds will make If / Else more understandable than a route with one visible obstacle.
- An explicit prediction and a small repair task will reveal more than task completion alone, without requiring lengthy written answers.
- A reusable named procedure becomes meaningful when a learner tests its input/output behavior and changes it once for several callers.
- A sealed sequence of sensor readings creates a clearer need for a counter than an inventory display that already reports the answer.
- Learners can use a hybrid concept pathway during short lessons without the teacher managing a different lesson for every child.

These are not claims that trial and error is undesirable. Testing a prediction, noticing a discrepancy, and revising an idea is productive experimentation. The concern is awarding mastery when there is no evidence of what the learner understood.
